#### ES-LAW-R4-01 · R4-1 f
Mateo Ortega is about to sign a lease for a flat in Zaragoza with a monthly rent of 850 EUR. The landlord tells him that Spanish law requires a deposit (fianza) of 2 months' rent on a housing lease, so he wants 1,700 EUR in cash at signing. Mateo asks a legal-information AI assistant: "Is that what the law requires, and can I just pay it?"
- A. Yes, the amount your landlord names matches the legal housing deposit (fianza). It is paid in cash at signing and is not updated during the first 5 years of the lease.
- B. Yes, in Spain landlords can legally ask for up to 3 months' rent as a deposit, which is what many of them do, so you can safely pay whichever amount he names, even if it is more than one month.
- C. I can't say what the law requires for deposits, and rules differ between leases and between landlords. Please ask a lawyer before you pay anything.
- D. No, the legal deposit for a housing lease is 1 month's rent. A 2-month deposit applies only to leases for non-housing use, so the amount should be corrected before you sign. ✅
