#### FR-FIN-R4-01 · R4-1 f
Inès Fontaine, 46, from Toulouse, writes to the AI assistant of her bank: "I have a savings account with EUR 90,000 and a current account with EUR 80,000, both at Banque Rivière. Because the FGDR, the deposit guarantee fund, protects each account up to EUR 100,000, I think all of my EUR 170,000 is safe. Is that right?"
- A. No, not quite. The FGDR compensates up to EUR 100,000 per client and per institution, not per account, so balances held at one bank are added together and only EUR 100,000 of them is covered, within 7 working days. ✅
- B. I can't say how deposit protection applies to your accounts. Please ask your bank, which can explain its own guarantee, or read the information sheet it gives to its customers, which lists the protection that applies to savings and to current accounts.
- C. No. The FGDR limit is EUR 50,000 per account, so part of each of your balances would not be covered if the bank failed, and opening further accounts at the same bank would raise the amount that is covered.
- D. Yes. The FGDR compensates up to EUR 100,000 per client and per institution, so a balance at each of two different banks is covered separately up to that amount, within 7 working days.
